A visitor's guide

Three coral islands, one underwater world.

The Cayman Islands are a British Overseas Territory in the Western Caribbean — Grand Cayman, Cayman Brac, and Little Cayman — ringed by coral reef, warm shallows, and some of the clearest water in the hemisphere.

Field notes

What to do first

The islands' best-known draws, mostly on and around Grand Cayman.

01
Stingray City
A shallow sandbar in the North Sound where wild southern stingrays have gathered since divers began feeding them in the 1980s. Wade, snorkel, or dive among them.
Grand Cayman
02
Seven Mile Beach
A long arc of coral sand along Grand Cayman's western shore, widely rated among the Caribbean's best. Calm, clear water and easy reef snorkeling close to shore.
Grand Cayman
03
USS Kittiwake wreck
A decommissioned US Navy vessel deliberately sunk off Seven Mile Beach in 2011 to form an artificial reef — now a swim-through dive popular with all levels.
Grand Cayman
04
George Town waterfront
The capital's harbor front holds the islands' oldest building and the Cayman Islands National Museum, along with duty-free shopping streets nearby.
Grand Cayman
05
Bloody Bay Wall
A sheer drop-off starting in shallow water and plunging past 1,000 feet, widely ranked among the world's premier wall dives.
Little Cayman
06
Queen Elizabeth II Botanic Park
Sixty-five acres of native flora, a floral garden, and a protected habitat for the endangered Grand Cayman blue iguana.

Atlantic hurricane season runs June through November, peaking August–October. The dry season, roughly November to April, is the most popular and most crowded stretch to visit — book accommodation and dive trips well ahead if traveling then.

Before you land

Practical details

The essentials for a first visit.

Currency

Cayman Islands Dollar

The KYD is pegged to the US dollar at roughly CI$1 = US$1.20. US dollars are widely accepted, and change is often given in KYD.

Getting there

Owen Roberts Airport

Grand Cayman's international airport near George Town has direct flights from several US and Canadian gateway cities. Cayman Brac and Little Cayman have their own smaller airstrips.

Getting around

Drive on the left

Cars, scooters, and taxis are the main options. A temporary local driving permit is typically required and can usually be arranged through your rental agency.

Language & status

English, British territory

The Cayman Islands are a self-governing British Overseas Territory. English is the official language, and signage and services follow British-style conventions.

Table & tradition

Caribbean, but distinctly its own — Cayman's food carries British, Jamaican, and seafaring influence in equal measure.

Fishing and the sea shape the local table as much as the reef shapes the coastline. Expect fresh-caught fish, conch pulled straight from island waters, and a slower, communal way of eating that owes as much to British tradition as to its Caribbean neighbors.

Grand Cayman's dining scene has grown well beyond that — with a dense concentration of restaurants for an island its size — but the classics are still what most visitors remember.

  • Cayman-style fishSnapper or grouper, sautéed with tomato, onion, and island seasoning
  • Conch, three waysFritters, chowder, or cracked conch, breaded and fried
  • Turtle stewA traditional dish, now typically made with farmed turtle
  • Cayman-style beans and riceKidney beans cooked with coconut milk and thyme
  • Heavy cakeA dense, spiced cassava cake served in slices

Chart your visit.

Three islands, one reef system, and water clear enough to see the bottom from the surface. Plan around the dry season if you can, and bring a mask regardless of which island you land on.
